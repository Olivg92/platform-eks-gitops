# 0008. What the demo API promises, and how it is measured

- **Status**: Accepted
- **Date**: 2026-09-23

## Context

An SLO is only useful if the numbers mean something. Two choices have to be made and justified:
what to promise, and how to measure it.

## Options considered

**What to measure**

1. **Uptime of the pods**: easy, and almost meaningless. A running pod that answers 500 is up.
2. **Request success ratio and latency, as seen by callers**: what users actually experience, and
   what an error budget can be built on.

**How to measure latency**

1. **A percentile** (`histogram_quantile(0.95, ...)`): the familiar p95, but it is interpolated
   between histogram buckets, and averaging percentiles over windows is mathematically wrong.
2. **A threshold count**: requests slower than 300ms counted as failures, read directly from the
   `le="0.3"` bucket. Exact, and it composes correctly over any window.

## Decision

Two objectives on the `/` endpoint, measured from the caller's point of view:

| Objective | Target | Error budget over 30 days |
|---|---|---|
| Availability: requests answered without a 5xx | 99.5% | about 3h36m of total failure |
| Latency: requests answered in under 300ms | 99% | about 7h12m worth of slow requests |

Latency is measured as a threshold count, not as a percentile. Percentiles are still plotted on the
dashboard, because they answer "how slow is slow" once the alert has fired.

The targets are deliberately modest. A 99.99% objective on a demo would produce an error budget so
small that any experiment blows it, and an SLO nobody can burn teaches nothing. These numbers leave
room to break the service on purpose, watch the budget drain, and see the alerts fire.

Alerting is multi-window burn-rate, generated by Sloth: a fast pair of windows pages, a slow pair
opens a ticket. Alerting on a raw error rate would page for every thirty-second blip and stay quiet
during a slow leak that eats the month's budget.

## Consequences

- The alerts carry a `runbook_url` annotation pointing at [docs/runbooks](../runbooks/), so the
  link is one click from the notification.
- The SLI queries have to tolerate an empty numerator: with no errors at all,
  `sum(rate(...))` returns no series and the ratio would not exist, so `or on() vector(0)` is
  part of the definition rather than a detail.
- **An SLI needs traffic to exist.** With no requests at all the ratio is 0/0, which Prometheus
  records as NaN, and Sloth builds its 30-day SLI as the average of the 5-minute ones, so a single
  NaN makes the error budget unreadable for the whole period. A one-request-per-second synthetic
  probe runs alongside the application to keep the denominator non-zero. Production platforms add
  blackbox probes for the same reason: "no traffic" is neither success nor failure.
- Metric labels stay low cardinality (route template, status class), because an SLI computed over
  an unbounded label set becomes both wrong and expensive.
- In production, the objectives would come from what the business can tolerate, not from what makes
  a good demo, and they would be reviewed when the budget is repeatedly missed.
